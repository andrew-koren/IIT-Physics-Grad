1\) Spring lattice discussion.

Page 1-3: 

$\omega$ dispersion relation, $\omega_0$ natural frequency of oscillators

Why is the dispersion relation called dispersion relation?
$$
    \text{classical: }E = {p^2 \over 2m}
\\  \text{quantum: }  \hbar \omega = {\hbar^2 k^2 \over 2m}
\\  \omega = {\hbar \over 2m} k^2
$$
A massive wave packet will spread (disperse) over time according to it's dispersion relation. In our QM class, etc. this is $\omega \propto k^2$, but a wavepacket in lattice spreads differently.

Phase velocity $\omega / k$. Group veloicty ${d \omega \over dk}$. We go on to disucss short/long wave approximation.

4\) Stress/Strain tensors

This starts as a thought experiment that a solid material functions like a continuous spring, an idea which is described by the stress/strain modulus (page 2), and ends up actually being the case in the real world.

Aside. In QM/stat mech and also classical, adiabatic changes refers to small probability to change between states but state energies change. Example: spring oscillating in mode but spring constant changes slowly.

Page 3-5:

Deformation inside a chunk of material. 

Instead of volume strain $\Delta V \over V$ we have strain field $\vec U(\vec r)$. 
We want translational/rotational invariance since we're in one material. Converting from antisymmetric to symmetric we get the strain tensor

$$
\epsilon_{ij} = {1 \over 2} \left( {\partial U_i \over \partial r_j} + {\partial U_j \over \partial r_i}\right)
$$
